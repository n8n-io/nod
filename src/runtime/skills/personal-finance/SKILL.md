# Personal finance

Define the accounts, period, currencies and result. Use read access for analysis. Distinguish actual balances, pending transactions and estimates. A transaction export can omit a linked account or a part of the period.

Clean records without changing the originals. Match transactions by source ID where possible. Transfers between the user's accounts are not new income or expenses. Credit-card payments can duplicate expenses already counted at purchase. Keep refunds, reversals and split transactions visible.

Use the user's category rules and show uncertain classifications. Reconcile opening balance, activity and closing balance for each account. Report gaps instead of forcing the totals to match. Do not add amounts in different currencies without a dated conversion basis.

For insurance, compare the same coverage, exclusions, limits, deductible, people and property. A quote can depend on facts that have not been confirmed. Do not cancel current coverage before the replacement's effective coverage is checked and the user has authorized the change.

For gift cards or credits, distinguish a discovered receipt from a checked remaining balance. A voucher can have region, merchant and expiry limits. Keep card numbers and access codes out of shared reports.

For payments, transfers, trades or product applications, use the exact current request and applicable action controls. Do not treat a savings recommendation as authority to move money. Financial and tax rules depend on jurisdiction and date; use current official sources for those rules. State what was checked and what still requires the user's or an expert's decision.
