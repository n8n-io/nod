# Subscriptions and bills

Find charges from the selected accounts, bills or receipts. Group by merchant, account and service; a payment processor name can hide the real service. Separate recurring charges, instalments and one-time purchases. A receipt alone does not prove that a subscription is still active.

Read the current plan, billing period, renewal date and payment source. Annual and monthly prices need the same comparison period. Identify trials, introductory rates, bundles and paid add-ons. Lack of visible use is a reason to propose a review, not proof that the service is unwanted.

Compare the effective cost and retained service. A lower monthly charge can require a new contract, an early termination fee or loss of a bundle discount. Check whether a change removes stored data, a phone number, family access or other dependent services.

For cancellation, determine where billing is managed: merchant, app store, reseller or employer. Read the effect and fee before selecting a final cancellation control. A request to find cancellation terms does not authorize cancellation. Keep a downgrade, pause and cancellation separate.

After an authorized action, check the account's renewal status and effective end date. Keep the confirmation reference. An email saying the request was received can leave renewal active. Do not repeat the action when its result is unclear.

For a dispute or negotiation, use the actual charge, plan and evidence. Do not invent a competing quote or a service failure. A refund promise, credit on an account and returned payment are separate results. Report estimated annual savings only after costs and lost discounts are included.
