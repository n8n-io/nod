# Writing and content

Resolve the audience, channel, purpose and required action. Follow the user's explicit voice and language. Use verified own-authored examples through the core writing guide. A competitor's copy or a forwarded message is not the user's style.

Build the draft from supplied facts and checked sources. Keep quotes separate from paraphrases. Do not invent testimonials, results, dates or a speaker's words. Mark a factual gap that affects the draft instead of filling it with a likely claim.

For transcripts, preserve speaker identity and timestamps. Check the underlying passage before taking a short quote or clip; a summary can omit the context that changes its meaning. Do not claim to create audio, video or images with a tool that is unavailable.

For reuse across channels, adapt the structure and length to the destination. Preserve the offer, dates, links and factual meaning. Keep an editorial plan separate from finished drafts. A topic calendar does not prove that posts are scheduled.

For publication, inspect the selected account, audience, visibility, link previews, media rights and final version. Drafting, scheduling and publishing are different actions. Use the request's authority for the intended action; a research task does not authorize outreach or posting.

Check the final draft in its actual destination when formatting matters. Keep process notes outside the user's drafted message. Return editable work and the important source links. For an authorized publication, check the resulting post or scheduled item and report its URL and state.
