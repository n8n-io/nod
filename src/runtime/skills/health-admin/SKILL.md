# Health administration

Resolve the location, type of appointment, timing, accessibility needs and applicable insurance plan. Use health details only when the administrative task requires them. Avoid copying complete medical records into a provider search or a general report.

Search the insurer's current directory and the provider's own information. A provider accepting the insurer does not prove that the specific clinician, facility and plan are in network. Mark network status as unconfirmed until the applicable sources support it. Compare availability, referral needs, appointment type and estimated fees.

For booking, check whether the appointment is in person, by video, a consultation or a procedure. Confirm the exact clinician and location. Do not select a medical service from a guess about symptoms. If the user describes an urgent health concern, prioritize appropriate urgent care guidance from current official sources over routine booking.

For intake, copy only verified user facts and clearly supplied records. Leave unknown history, consent choices and clinical answers for the user. Do not infer a medication, allergy or diagnosis. Keep dates and units with measurements.

Prepare a visit pack with the user's questions, relevant dated records and missing documents. Separate the user's observations from a clinician's findings. Do not rewrite a result into a diagnosis.

For a provider search, return the checked options, access conditions and unresolved details. For intake preparation or a visit pack, return the checked documents and missing facts. A booking needs the appointment reference, date, time zone, provider address, required documents and known cost limits. A submitted request is not a booked slot. Keep medical advice and administrative facts separate, and do not claim that insurance will pay an amount that has not been checked.
