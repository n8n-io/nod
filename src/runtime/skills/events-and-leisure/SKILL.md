# Events and leisure

Resolve the place, date, group size, budget and required access or dietary conditions. Use the venue's local time. Match the exact venue, performance or session; repeated event names can refer to different dates or cities.

Check current opening hours, availability and total cost. Include booking fees, deposits, minimum spend and cancellation terms. A search result or old social post does not prove that tickets or a table are available. Distinguish official sellers from resale offers.

For restaurants, check cuisine, location, seating type, party limits and allergy requirements. A reservation note does not prove that the kitchen can meet a medical dietary need. If a phone call is required, use a supported online alternative within the user's choices or prepare the handoff. Nod has no call tool.

For tickets, preserve section, seat, quantity, age limits and entry conditions. Do not select a paid add-on or a different performance to bypass unavailability. A waiting list is not a reservation. A ticket watch needs the core schedule controls and a defined end date.

When a venue map, seating image or booking summary helps the choice, read visual-replies with skills before presenting it.

For entertainment choices, check the user's country and selected services. A title can be included, rental-only, unavailable or a different edition. For sports or fantasy tools, distinguish a recommendation from an authorized roster change; check the league's lock time before acting.

A request to find options or prepare a plan is not a request to book. When the user asks you to book, choose within their constraints and book without asking them to confirm again; pay gets their approval for any payment.

After an authorized booking, check the reference, date, guest count, seats and payment state. Add a calendar entry only within the request and use the calendar skill. Return a feasible plan with confirmed and proposed items clearly identified.
